Report: Meeting of the Cultural Board on September 15, 2025

16.09.2025

The following topics were discussed at the meeting of the cultural board on September 15, 2025:

  • Savings in the 2025 and 2026 IKG budgets
  • Report from the anti-Semitism reporting center
  • Apartments for Jewish refugees from Ukraine
  • Election of vice presidents and commissions
  • Adaptation of the TMICHA statutes to new provisions on tax deductibility of donations
  • Activities for the anniversary October 7th
  • Restoration and renovation of the city temple
  • Inflation adjustment of cemetery fees

Savings in the 2025 and 2026 IKG budgets

 

At its last meeting before the summer break, the Board of Education commissioned the General Secretariat to look through the already approved budgets for 2025 and 2026 in consultation with the department heads for a total savings potential of 900,000 euros. As IKG President Oskar Deutsch explained again, this was made necessary on the one hand by the necessary additional spending on security and, on the other hand, by fixed and predicted loss of income from subsidies due to the austerity measures in the public sector. As Harald Sasse, IKG deputy general secretary responsible for finance, reported to the cultural board on Monday evening, this goal was achieved in close coordination with the department heads and commission chairs. Accordingly, the Finance Commission recommended a reduction in budgeted expenditure for 2025 by 464,950 euros and for 2026 by 435,800 euros.

Two thirds of this was achieved through savings from not filling planned additional jobs (such as in the anti-Semitism reporting center) or through more cost-effective filling of vacant positions, but also through the cancellation of further training courses and projects. Savings are also made in the area of ​​events (youth and culture), travel costs, consultations and small purchases. There are no cuts in the areas of social welfare or security. The majority of the Education Council approved the savings plan presented.

Report from the anti-Semitism reporting center

 

IKG General Secretary Benjamin Nägele introduced the new head of the anti-Semitism reporting center, Johannan Edelman, to the cultural board. He reported on a variety of incidents over the summer; some of them have already become known in the media. At the same time, he hears again and again, especially in personal conversations, that a lot of things are not being reported - given the large number of anti-Semitic incidents, a certain numbing and a feeling of normalization has occurred. He therefore appealed to everyone who is confronted with anti-Semitism to report it and emphasized that there are often more ways to take action against it than many people realize. At least such incidents are recorded in the reporting office's report - which in turn helps in discussions with the authorities and the government.

One case that particularly outraged this summer was the one in which an Uber driver refused to chauffeur a Jewish family. A report has been filed here and there has already been a conversation with Uber management. There will be consequences for the driver in question. It is a shame that in the wake of this incident it became known that some other Jews had problems with Uber drivers but did not report them. “It is incredibly important to report,” Edelman emphasized. This is possible either by telephone from Monday to Thursday from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. and Friday from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. ( +43 (1) 531 04-777), but you can also use the form on the reporting office's website around the clock ( https://www.antisemitismus-meldestelle.at. A basic principle of the reporting office is the protection of trust: the reporters decide how we will handle their report.

Apartments for Jewish refugees from Ukraine

 

IKG Secretary General Nägele informed the cultural board about a current problem. When Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022, many Ukrainians also fled to Vienna - including many Jews; At peak times, around 1,200 were comprehensively looked after by the IKG. With the help of many community members, the IKG immediately started the largest relief operation since the Shoah, organized meals in the community center, helped with psychosocial and unbureaucratic financial support and German courses, and the Tmicha association rented hundreds of apartments in a very tense housing market. All of this was covered by donations.

However, as of October 7, 2023, the willingness to donate to refugees from Ukraine has waned - the focus is now on helping Israel. In order to professionalize the care and support and to relieve the burden on the IKG and Tmicha, we cooperated with Volkshilfe and the “Fonds Soziales Wien” so that Volkshilfe provides apartments as part of the basic provision. Volkshilfe therefore took over Tmicha's rental agreements with the respective accommodation providers. To do this, however, it is necessary that the apartments on offer also meet the criteria set for basic services in terms of size and price. Some refugees from Ukraine have now moved on to other countries, especially Germany. Others would now be financially independent. Another part was convinced to move into the smaller apartments offered, the costs of which are fully covered by the basic services and do not represent any additional financial burden for the IKG/Tmicha.

However, a group of around 50 people continue to refuse to move out of the apartments originally rented by Tmicha, despite adequate alternative housing offers, which means that IKG/Tmicha continues to have to pay for the additional costs - currently almost 7,000 euros per month - which can no longer be covered by donations. The situation has even worsened in that this group is now represented by a lawyer who is suing both Tmicha and Volkshilfe. Both Nägele and Deutsch and Tom Zäuner, the head of the real estate department, emphasized that all those affected had been offered replacement apartments on several occasions, which would continue to enable them to live free of charge and provide comprehensive care (because basic services). It is hoped that a solution will be found here, but above all I should inform the cultural board so that they are informed accordingly when this group makes contact.

Election of vice presidents and commissions

 

At the halfway point of each legislative period there is a new election of the IKG vice presidents and the IKG commissions. However, the individual ballots hardly brought any surprises: Claudia Prutscher (Atid) and Michael Galibov (Jachad – VBJ) now hold the vice-presidential office as before. Both were elected with 16 out of a total of 18 votes cast. Here you will find an overview of all commission members and chairmen.

Adaptation of the TMICHA statutes to new provisions on tax deductibility of donations

 

Like the statutes of ESRA, the cultural board adapted the statutes of TMICHA to the new tax regulations on the deductibility of donations on the recommendation of a tax law firm. The term “charity” is now explicitly mentioned, and it is stated that the association's administrative costs may make up a maximum of ten percent of the donation income.

Activities for the anniversary October 7th

 

Secretary General Nägele informed about the plans to commemorate the victims of the Hamas terrorist attack on the Israeli civilian population around the anniversary of October 7th. The focus of a memorial event at Judenplatz, which will take place on October 5th due to the holiday, will be to mourn the victims. But further attention should also be drawn to the fate of the hostages. With a kind of sea of ​​lights - a loop of yellow candles should be laid - and the pictures of it, the aim is to send a signal to the affected families in Israel. There should be no politicians' speeches at this event; the focus should be on the families of those affected and the grief in the community. We cooperate here with youth organizations, with Bringhomenow.Vienna and many other Jewish and non-Jewish organizations. “We want to counteract the increasing lack of empathy among the non-Jewish population towards Israel, the hostages and the Jewish community.” A campaign with subjects on synagogues and other IKG properties will also contribute to this. Politicians and civil society organizations were also asked to send a sign of solidarity with the victims of October 7th and the hostages on their social media channels and in videos. A counterweight is needed here to the calls for a boycott against Israel across Europe. There will also be another commemoration in the synagogue.

Restoration and renovation of the city temple

 

As in the previous week at the Citizens' Parliament, KVin Natalie Neubauer, who with her architectural office KENH is also responsible for the renovation of the city temple starting after the holidays, informed the cultural board about the specific planned work. The modernization (technology, accessibility) is taking place in coordination with the Federal Monuments Protection Office.

IKG President Deutsch announced that there are now firm commitments from the City of Vienna and the Federal Government for a third of the total amount of 10.5 million euros each. With the donations already received, phase one of the project – the renovation and restoration of the city temple – has been financed and can be commissioned. The cultural board agreed to this on Monday evening.

Phase two will include the renovation of the community center and the facade. This should also be financed exclusively through third-party funds. The decision on this part of the work will only be made when the necessary funds are available.

Inflation adjustment of cemetery fees

 

The sharp increase in inflation is making it difficult to cover costs for the Jewish cemetery in Vienna, explained Secretary General Nägele. It is therefore necessary to increase cemetery fees. The cultural board agreed to this. The cheapest grave category (class 5), including burial costs, now comes to 2,800 euros (instead of the previous 2,600 euros). The most expensive graves (class 1) now cost 19,000 euros (instead of the previous 17,600 euros). Nägele emphasized that funerals in Vienna are significantly cheaper than in Munich (from 9,500 euros) or Frankfurt (from 4,400 euros). President Deutsch emphasized that it was important to him that favorable tariffs continue to be offered. This is only possible if the cemetery continues to operate in a cost-covering manner. An automatic mechanism was also decided upon so that whenever inflation exceeds five percent, the General Secretariat will carry out an indexation, of which the cultural board and subsequently the community members will be informed via the newsletter and the “Community Insider”.

The Statutes Commission is now expected to develop and present an amendment to the cemetery regulations by November. Also by November, the cultural board is expected to address the question of how the reservation or purchase of graves can be organized during one's lifetime. The current solution cannot be continued if people in Vienna - as before - strive for generally lower grave prices than in Jewish cemeteries in Germany or Switzerland.
