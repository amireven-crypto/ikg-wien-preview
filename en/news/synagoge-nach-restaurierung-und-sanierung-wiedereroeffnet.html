Synagogue reopened after restoration and renovation

10. 09. 26.
Share

Embedded media of the original page is not loaded in this demo (data protection - no request to third parties).

Open externally: embedded media (opens in new tab)

On September 9, 2026, two days before the start of the High Holidays, the Jewish community ceremoniously reopened its spiritual center in Seitenstettengasse after ten and a half months of construction.

IKG President Oskar Deutsch: "We are back home. The city temple was closed, our community life never was. We owe the fact that we are praying here again today to the Republic of Austria, the city of Vienna and hundreds of donors from the community and their friends at home and abroad. They have not only preserved a building, they have invested in the future of Jewish Vienna."

The synagogue was rededicated with a ceremonial insertion of a new Torah scroll. The Torah was brought from Judenplatz to the city temple in a procession. Around 450 community members celebrated the reopening there. The mezuzot were then attached - the parchment rolls with Torah verses that are attached to the door frames in Judaism.

Chief Rabbi Jaron Engelmayer: "The reopening of the city temple is a strong sign for the future. As the only active synagogue that survived the Shoah, the restoration is symbolically an investment in the future and Jewish life in Vienna and Austria."

In advance, the Austrian Post AG presented its own stamp to mark the 200th anniversary of the city temple and set up a special post office with a special stamp in Seitenstettengasse on September 9th. The stamp, designed by Melanie Mussegg, gives an insight into the interior of the synagogue, has a face value of 2 euros (tariff: International S Economy Welt) and is issued in a circulation of 150,000 pieces.

Image source: IKG/Rosa

Photos: IKG/Pink

The restored and renovated city temple

Building, security and fire protection measures, accessibility measures as well as a spatial reorganization of the entrance areas with a separate station for visitors of the guided tours (starting in winter 2026) were implemented. The Bima, the raised lectern from which the Torah is recited, and all surfaces were adapted true to the original. In addition, design components were realized based on Joseph Kornhäusel's design ideas.

Image source: IKG/Rosa

Photos: IKG/Pink

Historical building structures were also uncovered, such as the floor under the Bima made of Budapest marble and the stone base. All structural measures were carried out by KENH Architects in close coordination with the Federal Monuments Office and the technical department of Israelitische Kultusgemeinde Wien (Jewish Community of Vienna). In addition to the historical appearance of the room and the improved acoustics, the restoration and renovation serve to modernize the community infrastructure: The city temple will be fit for the future for future generations - and will remain an open house that makes its contribution to coexistence in Vienna and Austria.

The construction work took place within the planned ten and a half months. In the following phases, the community center and the facade of the city temple will be renovated. The total cost of the restoration and renovation is 10.5 million euros. A third each comes from the federal government, the city of Vienna and private donations. Around 95% of the total costs have already been raised

Image source: IKG/Morgensztern

About the city temple

The City Temple is the spiritual center of the Jewish community in Vienna and the largest synagogue in Austria. It is the only synagogue in Vienna that survived the November pogroms in 1938 and is still in continuous use today.
