Report: Meeting of the Cultural Board on October 12, 2026

13. 10. 26.
Share

The following topics were discussed at the meeting of the cultural board on October 12, 2026:

  • General meeting JBBZ
  • Political situation in Austria
  • President's report

General meeting JBBZ

271 people are currently attending a training program offered by the Jewish Vocational Training Center (JBBZ), 48 of them at the Gredlerstrasse location. In three weeks, with the start of a new Matura group, there will be 280 - a high, as the educational director of the JBBZ, Klaus Bruckner, reported to the cultural board on Monday evening.

He also leads the one-year career orientation course, the so-called “9th school year”. It was important to him to convey in his report: Yes, sometimes everyday life “doesn’t always run smoothly”. We work here with young people who are not taught further by other schools because, for example, they have behavioral problems or are not cognitively strong enough. But at the JBBZ, with a lot of patience in small group lessons and thanks to the support of ESRA, they managed to get them to graduate school. In the Jewish community there are then derogatory words due to one or two incidents that become known. Bruckner said anyone can contact him at any time to get first-hand information if the case arises. But he is especially proud when he looks at the careers that people who have completed this course have now had, including those who now work at the Maimonides Center and others who have completed university studies.

The JBBZ is also proud of those who pass the Matura as part of the “vocational maturity test” offer. The success rate here is recently at 100 percent, reported Susanne Quehenberger, head of the language and special training department. Anyone who has vocational training, for example in the form of an apprenticeship or a technical school, can take the Matura with a focus on media information technology in one year. The JBBZ team strives to provide tailor-made preparation for the exams, which will then be taken at the HTL Spengergasse.

Ten students from the Gredlerstrasse location also recently successfully passed their first Matura exams, reported the head there, Rabbi Yaacov Frenkel (Kehille). You previously attended the Malzgasse school. If they have passed all the exams at the end, they would also have access to a university at home or abroad. Frenkel was also proud that some graduates had already founded their own companies.

The JBBZ Gredlerstraße will celebrate its tenth anniversary next February. The JBBZ on Adalbert Stifter-Straße can now look back on several decades of training activities. In addition to a permanent contract for a period of 30 years, the framework agreement with the Employment Service (AMS) stipulated that this could only be terminated for special reasons, for example if the JBBZ was guilty of misconduct. These 30 years are now coming to an end, reported managing director Markus Meyer. The permanent contract will remain in place from 2027 - and that in itself is something special. Many institutions would like to have such a contract. There will also be another change from next year: Inter-company apprenticeship training will no longer be carried out via the City of Vienna's Municipal Department 5 (MA 5), as was previously the case, but via the Vienna Employees' Promotion Fund (WAFF).

The 2025 annual financial statements were presented by Petra Grätz from Merkur Treuhand, which is responsible for an audit every year (in addition to the audit by treasurer Nechemja Gang). The total income amounted to 5.4 million euros (mainly this came from the AMS and the City of Vienna), the largest expense item is personnel costs. The provisions for the anniversary bonus for long-term employees newly provided for in the BABE collective agreement (the KV for employees in private educational institutions) were also taken into account here.

Club chairman Dezoni Dawaraschwili (VGJ) thanked the JBBZ team as well as IKG President Oskar Deutsch for the quality of the training offer, the excellent functioning of daily operations and the good cooperation with the IKG and the cultural board. Deutsch also submitted an application for the discharge of the JBBZ board, which was unanimously accepted by the cultural board.

Political situation in Austria

On the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York in September, Federal President Alexander Van der Bellen spoke of “violations of international law” and “war crimes” by Israel. Deutsch commented on this in a reaction that was widely discussed in the media in Austria, but also by some media in Israel, as he reported to the cultural board:

In his statement shared on social media, Deutsch criticized Van der Bellen for “taking sides.” Precisely because the Federal President has always been a friend of Israel, his words would carry even greater weight. The fact that Van der Bellen did not say a word about the existential threat posed by Palestinian terrorist organizations as well as the Iranian regime and its proxies was also the subject of criticism. “He did not speak of the attacks on Israelis or the declared goal of exterminating the Jews,” said Deutsch’s statement. “When a head of state presents one-sided accusations as facts, he legitimizes those who already “knew” who was to blame.

Deutsch also reported on an appearance by the two FPÖ politicians Herbert Kickl (federal party chairman) and Harald Vilimsky (head of the EU delegation) in Strasbourg, where a conference of the Patriot group in the EU Parliament took place in mid-September. The topic “Islamism and anti-Semitism: threats to Europe’s freedom” was discussed. What Kickl said there were words that “some of them are things you like to hear,” said Deutsch. At the same time, Vilimsky attacked the IKG and an English translation of Kickl's speech was distributed in Israel, which contained an apology to Jews for the Holocaust. But in his lecture, which was given in German, Kickl regretted what had “happened” to the Jews, but he did not apologize. However, the Israeli Diaspora Minister received these falsely transmitted quotes favorably and praised them on his social media.

In the subsequent debate, representatives of many factions recalled anti-Israel and anti-Semitic statements from the ranks of the FPÖ.

President's report

As chairman of the Finance Commission, Deutsch reported that General Secretary Benjamin Nägele and his deputy Harald Sasse had been in discussions with all departments and commission chairmen regarding budget matters since the beginning of the summer. Efforts are being made to achieve a zero deficit for the upcoming double budget, but we are not there yet. The Finance Commission will meet again in ten days. The current debt repayment plan is currently being worked on and will then be presented together with the budget for the next two years.

In his role as IKG President, Deutsch reported on the extensive celebrations on the occasion of the reopening of the city temple, at which leading politicians such as Chancellor Stocker and Vienna's Mayor Michael Ludwig supported the Jewish community. That is not a given.

In a conversation with EU Commissioner Magnus Brunner, he suggested that the Commission should support the Jewish communities in Europe through the governments in the areas of security and in the fight against anti-Semitism. He also suggested a media campaign to combat anti-Semitism, said Deutsch. He also regretfully reported that the European Commission's long-time coordinator for combating anti-Semitism and promoting Jewish life, Katharina von Schnurbein, was leaving this position; the successor was still open. Schnurbein is now taking over the position of anti-Semitism commissioner for the federal government in Germany.

During a visit by Health Minister Korinna Schumann, the discussion with ESRA chairwoman and KVin Dwora Stein (Federation) discussed the ministry's cooperation with ESRA.

Otherwise, Deutsch reported on the Rosh Hashanah receptions by Centropa and State Secretary Alexander Pröll as well as a “Council of Religions” with Mayor Ludwig, where – among other topics – Chief Rabbi Yaron Engelmayer, who has now left Vienna, was given a very dignified farewell.
