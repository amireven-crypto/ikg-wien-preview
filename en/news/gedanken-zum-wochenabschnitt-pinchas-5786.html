Thoughts on the weekly section - Pinchas 5786

30. 06. 26.
Share

by Chief Rabbi Jaron Engelmayer

Why does G"d need atonement?

Even from kindergarten, many Jewish children are familiar with the story of how the moon was reduced in size when the world was created. This is based on a midrash we encountered this week in the Daf Hayomi (daily study of one sheet of Talmud): (bab. Talmud Chulin 60b) "And G"d made the two great luminaries, the great luminary and the small luminary (Genesis 1:16). Said the moon to the lord of the world: Can two kings rule with one crown? Then He answered him: Go, diminish yourself... Thus said the Holy One, blessed be His name (to the Jewish people): Make atonement for Me, because I have diminished the moon. So Resch Lakisch said: How does the goat as an atonement sacrifice on the new moon differ (from those of other holidays) that it says "for the Eternal" (Numbers 28:15)? Thus said the Holy One, blessed be His name: This he-goat is a propitiation for Me, because I have diminished the moon."

What is this atonement that Resch Lakisch refers to in our weekly section? Does G"d need atonement? And why is this atonement referred to in the Mussaf prayer of Rosh Chodesh as a "time of reconciliation for all generations/descendants "? According to various passages in the Talmud (Chagiga 12a, Taanit 27b), this atonement is said to have a beneficial effect on the small children, i.e. the "offspring", and to protect them. What is the connection between them and the atonement on the day of the new moon? We would actually assume that small children are more innocent than adults, and if one day of atonement a year - Yom Kippur - is enough for them, then not even more so for the children. So why monthly atonement?

Rav Nachum L. Rabinovitch gives a special explanation for these questions and the symbolism behind them: The Creator created two large celestial bodies. The sun has its cycle once a year and creates the seasons. However, there is no beginning or end to this cycle, so it appears to be very slow and constant. In contrast, the cycle of the moon is much shorter, monthly, and has a clear beginning and end, with the lunar renewal towards the new moon.

The situation is similar with nature, which God gave to humans: adults - similar to the sun - are mostly in a fairly constant life routine and behavior. If these are already good habits, this has many advantages, but it is more difficult to change them, since every change goes against stability and constancy in life. There is no clear beginning for this and no recognizable new beginning in nature.

The situation is different for children and younger generations: their lives are dynamic and subject to constant change. Stability is relative, everything can change and redesign quite easily. What seemed acceptable and good last month can be discarded and completely rethought this month. Today you're passionate about a certain idea and tomorrow you'll have other goals in mind. Sometimes this can depend on different moods.

The advantage of this is that bad paths can be quickly abandoned. On the other hand, there needs to be continuity and reliability in life, a commitment to medium and long-term goals that are not lost sight of - unlike the moon, which is constantly renewing itself. That's why the moon needs atonement, that's why the Creator, who has given man's nature different sides, "needs" atonement to make people aware that changes are sometimes good and necessary, but consistency and long-termism are essential in the pursuit of great goals and must not suffer from their continuity or lose constancy.
