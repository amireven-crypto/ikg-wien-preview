Ceremony of the Republic in the reopened city temple

17. 09. 26.

On Wednesday evening, September 16th, the reopened Vienna City Temple became the scene of a special ceremony. Representatives of the Republic of Austria and the City of Vienna as well as numerous guests gathered in Seitenstettengasse to celebrate the reopening of the city temple together. Numerous media representatives attended the ceremony on site, while ORF III broadcast the celebrations live, thereby making the reopening accessible to a broad audience. The ceremony was also a highlight in the synagogue's anniversary year, which this year looks back on 200 years of history.

the ceremony in full length on ORF on

The Chief Cantor opened with Shmuel Barzilai and the Tempelchor, accompanied by Dror Binder. A musical beginning that anticipated the character of the evening: a historic place reopened its doors after months of restoration and renovation.

Timna Brauer led the ceremony. After the welcome by the moderator, IKG President Oskar Deutsch addressed the guests and addressed the increasing anti-Semitism and the special security precautions that are necessary today so that Jewish life and Jewish places can be lived and visited safely. At the same time, he emphasized the deep connection between Judaism and Austria: "Jewish life already existed here in Roman times. Jewish communities have existed for almost 1,000 years. That's why let me say one thing: Judaism belongs to Austria like milk to melange."

The joint ribbon cutting marked a visible moment of reopening. Federal President Alexander Van der Bellen, IKG President Oskar Deutsch, Chief Rabbi Jaron Engelmayer and the two vice presidents of IKG Claudia Prutscher and Michael Galibov, sent a symbolic signal for a new beginning in a place that is also deeply rooted in Vienna's history.

The fact that the city temple is much more than just a historical building became clear as the evening progressed. The focus was on the history of the synagogue, which was the only one of Vienna's once numerous synagogues to survive the November pogroms in 1938 and is still in continuous use today.

In his speech, Chancellor Christian Stocker underlined the importance of the City Temple and Jewish life for the Republic of Austria: "The Vienna City Temple has stood in the heart of this city for 200 years now. Since its opening in 1826, it has been the spiritual center of the Jewish community in Vienna, a place of prayer, encounters and community life. Its history is inseparably linked to the history of Austria."

A special moment of the ceremony was the prayer for the well-being of the state of Austria and for peace around the world, which Chief Rabbi Engelmayer said. This was followed by Chief Cantor Barzilai, accompanied by Dror Binder, with the verse from Isaiah 56:7: “…for my house shall be called a house of prayer for all nations.”

In his speech, Mayor Michael Ludwig also emphasized the importance of cohesion in Vienna and also that home is where there is security. Vienna shows that this good coexistence works and that we don't allow ourselves to be divided. In this way, Jewish life in Vienna could also flourish.

At the end, the festive character of the evening was once again recorded musically by Chief Cantor Barzilai, the temple choir and the children's choir. With this act, the ceremonial act of the Republic ended in the reopened city temple.

A house with history and future

The celebrations followed directly on from the reopening of the city temple on September 9th. After ten and a half months of construction, the Jewish community was able to move back into its spiritual center in Seitenstettengasse in time for the High Holidays. Around 450 community members had already taken part in the ceremonial introduction of a new Torah scroll at the reopening.

During the restoration and renovation, not only building, security and fire protection measures were implemented and accessibility was improved. The bima and the surfaces of the interior were also adapted true to the original. Historical building structures, including the floor under the Bima made of Budapest marble and historical stone bases, were made visible again. At the same time, the infrastructure of the city temple was modernized and the acoustics improved.

The reopened city temple thus combines its historical substance with the requirements of a lively community. The total costs for the restoration and renovation amount to 10.5 million euros, one third of which is covered by the federal government, the city of Vienna and private donations. Around 95% of the total costs have already been raised.
