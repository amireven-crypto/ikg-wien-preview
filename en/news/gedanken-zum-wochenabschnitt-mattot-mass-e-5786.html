Thoughts on the weekly section - Mattot-Mass`e 5786

07. 07. 26.
Share

by Chief Rabbi Jaron Engelmayer

The real problem of denial of service

Moshe found unusually sharp words for the concerns of the tribes of Reuwen and Gad. Actually, they came very respectfully and formulated an understandable request, which even had an advantage for everyone involved: (Numbers 32, 4-5) "The land which the LORD has struck before the community of Israel is a pastureland, and your servants (we) have flocks. And they said: If we have found favor in your eyes, may this land be given to your servants as a possession, do not lead us over the Jarden."

And yet Moshe spoke out against them to an unusual extent and with even greater severity (there 6-15): How could it be that their brothers were going to battle and they were resting here? He accused them of apostasy and rebellion so that they would cause all the Israelites to turn away! But that was just the beginning, for now he began to compare them to the spies and their followers who led their entire generation to death in the desert, and concluded that the supplicants would also lead the entire people straight into the abyss!

Only the declaration by the two tribes that they would go to war before their brothers could bring about appeasement and pave the way to an agreement between Moshe and the tribes.

What aroused Moshe's anger so much? In particular, according to the commentators, that the two tribes planned to go to war from the outset and just didn't mention it directly (Ramban, Abarbanel), but also according to the commentators that they actually had no intention of doing so at first, and only after Moshe's words gave in and agreed to take part in the war - was that a reason to treat them so harshly and immediately compare them with the spies?

Yes, it was. The practical problem was not just the possibility that the Israelite army would be extremely weakened if these two strong tribes did not fight. After the brilliant and miraculous victories over the Midjanites, who were inflicted a great and just defeat without a single loss, and over the all-powerful kings Sichon and Og, who went into battle against them, it was incomprehensible how they could now avoid this task when God had shown such open support to the Israelites.

But the real problem was actually much deeper: if the two tribes had not gone to war with their brothers, they would have caused a deep and perhaps insurmountable division among the entire people! They would have shown: we have received our property, the fighting is over for us, from now on you are on your own. If every tribe acted like this, with purely sectoral interests, the people as a whole would have nothing left, neither in the fight nor afterwards. Because that would mean that the entire foundation of Israel's community of solidarity, cohesion and, above all, common destiny would have been completely lost.

And yes, even after Ramban and Abarbanel's declaration that they never intended to shy away from the fight, their words were still abundantly clear, because the fact that they "forgot" to mention this was not a mere mishap, but showed that it was not particularly important to them and was not on their hearts. Much more important to them were the pastures and the possession of the already conquered lands, which alone they spoke of at first.

These tendencies are extremely dangerous for the present and, above all, the common future of the Jewish people, and were therefore promptly and strongly condemned by Moshe.
