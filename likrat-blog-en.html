Likrat Blog

Welcome to “Likrat – Let’s talk CLEARLY!”

In our column, our Likratinos*as give very personal insights: They share experiences, thoughts and talk openly about what Jewish life means to them - honestly, diversely and directly from everyday life. Here, young Jews speak CLEAR TEXT – about identity, encounters and everything that moves them.

Straight talk with Rachel

April 2025

Rachel, 23 years old, Likratina since 2020

In this issue, Likratina Rachel shares her story:

When I started at Likrat five years ago, I could never have imagined being part of this project for so long. Likrat is ultimately based on the peer-to-peer principle: the goal is to send Likratinos and Likratinas of the same age into school classes in order to enable encounters on an equal footing.

But over time, not only have I changed, but so has Likrat. From the classic encounters with students, many other formats have emerged over the years: Today we also speak with students, teachers, police officers in training and refugees (with the support of interpreters). We do this because we realize that the topics we talk about are relevant to all age groups.

When I am asked why I am still active in Likrat even though I am very busy with my studies and my job, I talk about the moments at the end of every encounter. From people who were initially unsure or even skeptical, who didn't know what to expect and who may have never spoken to a Jewish person before. And who smile at me at the end and thank me for my courage, my openness and for the time I gave them.

I’m not just committed to “the others”. I do it for US too. Likrat also strengthens from within - This is important especially in a time when news and media are circulating at lightning speed through social media and opinions are being shaped by it.

At Likrat many people meet a Jewish person for the first time; it is their first contact with real and authentic Judaism. Of course you can't really get to know anyone in an hour, but sometimes a single conversation and the moment is enough to show that we are only human. You don't have to be afraid to ask questions and be curious. Openness can open doors you never knew existed.

Plain text with Amit

March 2025

Amit, 17 years old, Likratina in training:

In this issue, Likratina, in training, shares her thoughts with Amit on the topic of normalizing “being Jewish in Vienna”.

I moved from Israel to Vienna when I was four. Initially my parents were looking for an Austrian kindergarten for my siblings and me, but unlike the average Austrian toddler, I didn't have blue eyes or blonde hair, just brown curls and no understanding of the German language. Finally, my parents decided to send me to ZPC. We are not a religious family, so I first learned about a lot of things there. But I looked like the other children, sometimes even Hebrew was spoken and I soon had German under control.

The Jewish community in Vienna is like a bubble: in the middle of it, it's very easy to lose sight of the outside world, the rest of Vienna - and if you don't look for it as an outsider, you won't notice the community.

After elementary school, I moved to an Austrian school about 30 minutes from the city center. There were many Christians and a few Muslims in my class. Over the course of the next seven years, I lost contact with the Jewish community, including a few family friends. It happened slowly, but over time I realized how much I missed it and how lonely you can feel when you realize that in everyday life there is no one who is also Jewish, or at least knows something about it.

Last year a friend told me about her experience in Likrat. After a lot of back and forth, I registered for the class of 2025. At the beginning I was afraid that I wouldn't fit in because I had already forgotten a lot and didn't know my way around very well, but I made friends very quickly and learned a lot from the seminars. Suddenly there were people around me with whom I shared culture and I no longer had to think twice before introducing myself as an Israeli Jew. A friend I met in Likrat invited me to a youth organization, BBYO. I found a second family there too.

Over the past six months, thanks to Likrat, I have integrated myself into the Jewish community and it has really replaced a kind of emptiness in me. The normalization of Judaism in everyday Austrian life is exactly what I want to achieve with Likrat - so that as a Jew you don't feel alone even outside the community.

Plain text with Julia

December 2024

Julia, 21 years old, Likratina since 2023:

In this issue, Likratina Julia shares her thoughts on the topic of integration:

I am constantly reminded that our similarities are far stronger than our differences. As Likratinos and Likratinas, we dare to step into the unknown when we enter spaces like the Austrian Integration Fund (ÖIF). For me, this step is always associated with excited curiosity and almost childlike enthusiasm. Because no matter how an encounter goes, I know: I will learn something new and take away unforgettable memories.

I think back to an encounter at the ÖIF that was initially marked by noticeable tension. We were Jews - often portrayed in the media in a way that makes us seem alien and distorted. Many questions arose: about Judaism, about our everyday life in Vienna, about the current war. But with every minute the atmosphere in the room became more relaxed. In the end, the initial tension dissipated like fog. This happened because our conversation brought us closer and created a warmth that had been missing before.

The meeting at the ÖIF ended with smiles, hugs and a deep feeling of connection. A Syrian participant said at the end that we, the Jewish people, do not correspond to the image that the media - especially in the Middle East - often paints. Such moments show that peaceful coexistence is possible. It is these encounters in which words build bridges and warmth dissolves tensions. After each encounter, I feel a flame of joy and pride ignite within me because I see how Likrat's work is making the world a little warmer and brighter.

It makes me all the more proud that Likrat was awarded the Integration Prize of the Austrian Integration Fund (ÖIF) in the “Commitment against Anti-Semitism” category. This honor recognizes our ongoing work and sends a clear message: intercultural dialogue must be further promoted in order to reduce prejudices and strengthen cooperation.
